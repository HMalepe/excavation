## Part 2 — Grade 9 Foundation Report (sample)

### "You've been interviewing people since you were eight. You just didn't call it that."

---

**Your shape**

*What you'd love doing.* You light up when you get to figure out why things happen, when you're helping someone through something, and when you're making something original. Selling, admin and spreadsheets pull you much less. Your three strongest pulls are thinking, helping and creating, in that order.

*How you're wired.* You're very curious, and warm in a way people notice. You feel pressure strongly, so deadlines land heavier on you than on some of your friends. Your follow-through is in the middle: you finish what matters to you, and drift on what doesn't.

*What matters most.* Looking after your people comes first. Close behind is freedom to choose your own path. Those two can pull against each other, and you'll feel that more over the next few years.

---

**Who you are**

You said you used to make whole magazines for your cousins, "with interviews and drawings". That's the clearest picture in your whole session. Asking questions, listening, then making something out of what you heard. You still do it. "People tell me their problems. I don't mind, I actually like it." Plenty of people find that draining. You find it interesting.

Your answers say you're a thinker first. But you're not a lab-coat thinker. You think about *people*: why they do what they do, and what would help. That's a specific kind of mind, and it fits more futures than you might expect.

There's one thing worth being honest about. You sounded sure about medicine, then said: "Doctor was my gran's idea. I think. I never really asked myself." That's not a problem. Plenty of people end up loving the plan their family hoped for. But right now it's your gran's plan that you're carrying, not yet one you've tested yourself.

---

**How you work**

You work hardest when something matters to you, not when someone is watching. That's a strength, and it means boring subjects will feel twice as heavy. You believe effort makes you better, which matters more than any mark right now. Your study habits are in the middle: you don't always have a set time and place, and you tend to re-read notes instead of testing yourself. That's the easiest thing in this report to fix.

---

**Your subject options**

**1. Humanities & Social Sciences** (History, Geography, Life Sciences, with Mathematics)
This is the closest match to how you think and what you care about. History and Life Sciences were two of the three subjects you picked when we took the pressure away. It keeps psychology, social work, law, journalism and teaching open. With Maths instead of Maths Lit, commerce stays open too.
*Closes:* medicine, engineering and most pure science degrees, which need Physical Sciences.

**2. Visual Arts & Design** (Visual Arts, Design, History, with Mathematics)
Your Creative Arts mark is your highest, and the magazine you made at eight wasn't an accident. This combination suits the maker in you: design, media, architecture-adjacent work, and creative careers that involve people.
*Closes:* medicine, engineering and the sciences. Some creative courses need a portfolio, so start keeping your work now.

**3. Health & Life Sciences** (Physical Sciences, Life Sciences, Geography, with Mathematics) — *keeps the most doors open*
You're capable here: your Natural Sciences mark is strong. This keeps medicine open, as well as occupational therapy, psychology, pharmacy and every science degree. It's the safest choice for options, and the hardest workload of the three.
*Before choosing it, ask yourself one question:* would you pick Physical Sciences if your family had never mentioned medicine?

**Be careful with: Commerce & Finance** — *competence trap*
You told us you're good at Accounting and you hate it. Your mark will probably follow you into Grade 10, but your energy won't. You could do well here, but it's likely to drain you.

**Maths:** take Mathematics, not Maths Literacy. Your 58% is enough, and it keeps far more doors open. Requirements differ between universities and change every year, so check the prospectus.
**Check with your school** which of these combinations it actually offers.

---

**The pattern to watch: choosing for approval**

You care deeply about your people, and you'd rather carry their plan than disappoint them. That's love, and it can also quietly make your choices for you. You'll know it's happening when you say yes to something and feel tired straight after. When you notice that feeling, ask one question out loud: "Is this mine, or am I carrying it for someone?"

---

**Your first move (this week)**

Ask your gran why she thought of medicine for you. Not to argue, just to understand. Write her answer down, then write yours underneath it.

---

**The question we'll ask you in Grade 11**
"Is the plan you're living now one you chose, or one you carried?"

### You were making magazines about people before anyone told you what a career was. Start there.

## Part 4 — Grade 11 Confirmation Report (sample)

### "Two years ago you were carrying a plan. Now you're choosing one."

---

**Two years: what stayed**
- You're still a thinker first. Your curiosity hasn't dropped at all.
- Looking after your people and freedom to choose your path are still your top two values.
- You still feel pressure strongly before tests.

**Two years: what moved**
- **Helping people overtook thinking** as your strongest interest. That's a real shift, and it fits everything you said today.
- Your follow-through jumped. You said it yourself: "I actually plan."
- "Fairness for everyone" entered your top three values.

**Who you're becoming:** in Grade 9 you'd accepted a plan without testing it. Now you're questioning it, and you went and job-shadowed at an OT practice to find out. That's what testing a plan looks like.

---

**Your subjects, honestly**
- **Life Sciences** — fits and working. Your best mark and your favourite class.
- **Geography** — fits and working. Solid.
- **Mathematics** — working. Not your favourite, but steady, and you need it.
- **Physical Sciences** — fits, but struggling. You dropped from 52% to 41%. You told us you only study it before tests. That's the cause, and it's fixable.

---

**Verdict: RESCUE — right subjects, fixable problem**

Your subjects fit where you're heading. Both psychology and occupational therapy are open from here, and OT typically needs Physical Sciences, so switching would close a door you're actively considering. The problem isn't the subject. It's how you're studying it: last-minute only, which works for Life Sciences because you love it, and fails for Physical Sciences because you don't. Your follow-through has grown by a lot since Grade 9. Point it at this one subject for one term and see what moves.

---

**Your pathway**
- **On your current marks you're tracking towards a Bachelor's pass.** Keep it.
- **For OT, Physical Sciences matters.** Aim for 50%+ by the June exams; many programmes want higher. *Check the requirements at the universities you're considering, because they vary and change every year.*
- **Your generic APS is about 28** (best six, excluding LO). Each university calculates it differently.
- **Most universities make provisional offers on Grade 11 final marks**, so the next nine months count more than any other stretch of school.

---

**Your plan**

| Action | By when | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Swap re-reading for self-testing in Physical Sciences: close the book, write what you remember, check it | Starting this week | You |
| Three fixed 30-minute Physical Sciences slots a week, same time and place, one past-paper question each | Every week until the June exams | You |
| Show your Physical Sciences teacher the two topics that feel shakiest and ask for one worked example of each | Before the end of Term 1 | You + subject teacher |
| Talk to one psychologist and one OT about their actual week | Before the June holidays | You (LO teacher can help find contacts) |
| Decide: psychology or OT | June holidays (your words) | You |

---

**The pattern to watch: last-minute studying for subjects you don't love**
It works when you care, and it costs you when you don't. The early warning sign: you open Physical Sciences notes for the first time the night before a test. Counter-move: book the three slots in your phone now, before you feel like it.

---

**Your first move (this week)**
Put three 30-minute Physical Sciences slots in your phone calendar, then do the first one before Sunday.

### At eight you made magazines about people's lives. At sixteen you went to find out what helping them actually looks like. Keep going.

## Part 7 — Visual layout notes for the app

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Headline | Alone on screen for ~1.5 seconds, then the "shape" visual draws in (never pops in) |
| Interests | Hexagon (R-I-A-S-E-C) drawn as a shape, labelled with friendly names (Builder, Thinker, Creator, Helper, Leader, Organiser) |
| Grade 11 deltas | The Grade 9 hexagon in grey under the Grade 11 one, so the change is visible at a glance |
| Subject options | Three cards with a "doors open / doors closed" strip each |
| Verdict | One large word (CONFIRM / RESCUE / RETHINK / ADJUST ONE / REDIRECT) with its one-line headline |
| Plan | Table with tick boxes the learner can check off; dates sync to their calendar if they allow it |
| Closing line | Last element on the page. Nothing after it: no "thank you", no share prompt |
| Download | PDF export of the full report; the teacher and parent summaries export separately |
