## Part A — The one-page proposal

### The Excavation: following learners from subject choice to university application

**The problem**
Grade 9 learners choose the subjects that decide which university doors stay open, usually based on what parents want, what friends choose, or what sounds impressive. By Grade 11 many are in subjects that don't fit them, and most universities make provisional offers on Grade 11 final marks. Current career-guidance tools measure aptitude once and never check whether the advice held up.

**What The Excavation does**
Two short sessions, two years apart:

1. **Grade 9 Foundation** (~90 min): a scored questionnaire built on established career and personality research, followed by a guided conversation. Each learner receives 3 subject combinations that fit them, the university doors each one keeps open or closes, and an honest Maths vs Maths Literacy recommendation.
2. **Grade 11 Confirmation** (~75 min): the same questionnaire again, plus how each subject is actually going. Each learner receives a clear recommendation: confirm, rescue a struggling subject, rethink the career target, or change a subject while the school still allows it, with dated actions and their projected pass type.

**Why it's different**
- It measures the same learner twice, so it can show whether its Grade 9 advice was right.
- It separates "wrong subject" from "fixable study problem", so learners don't switch when they should get support.
- Teachers receive a one-page summary per learner (with the learner's consent) that tells them what would help most.

**Safeguards**
POPIA-compliant guardian consent · no clinical or trauma content · AI disclosed to learners · conversation content private to the learner · a safeguarding protocol with a named contact at every school · reviewed by a registered psychologist · results reported to schools only in groups of 10 or more.

**The proposed pilot**
Free for participating schools in 2027. Three schools, about 300 learners per touchpoint. The department receives an anonymised outcomes report.

## Part B — Pilot design

### Timeline (2027 school year)

| When | What | Learners |
|---|---|---|
| Nov–Dec 2026 | Attorney + clinical advisor sign-off; research approval through the department's process; school agreements | — |
| **Term 1 2027 (weeks 3–6)** | **Pilot 1: Grade 11 Confirmation** (standalone, no Grade 9 baseline). Immediate value: catches learners while subject changes may still be possible and before Grade 11 finals | ~300 Grade 11s across 3 schools |
| Term 2 2027 | Interim report to schools and the department | — |
| **Term 2–3 2027** | **Pilot 2: Grade 9 Foundation**, at least 4 weeks before each school's subject-choice deadline | ~300 Grade 9s across the same 3 schools |
| Term 4 2027 | Pilot report: completion, usefulness, safeguarding, early outcomes | — |
| 2028–2029 | Follow-up: the 2027 Grade 9s take Grade 11 Confirmation in Term 1 2029 | Same cohort |

### Suggested school mix
One township school, one suburban school, one technical or focus school. That covers different subject offerings, data access and language profiles, and makes the findings credible.

### What we measure

| Measure | How | When |
|---|---|---|
| Completion rate and time taken | System logs | Every session |
| Learner clarity | "How clear are you about your subject choice / direction?" (1–10) before and after | Every session |
| Usefulness | Learner rating + LO teacher rating of summaries | 2 weeks after |
| Identity status | Distribution and movement (exploring and committing vs drifting) | G9, G11 |
| Rescue effectiveness | Mark change by June exams for subjects that received a Rescue plan | Grade 11 pilot |
| Subject changes | Number of changes made, and whether they followed a Redirect/Adjust verdict | Grade 11 pilot |
| Safeguarding | Soft/hard triggers, school response times | Continuous |
| **Long-term (2029+)** | Grade 11 verdict distribution for learners who had the Grade 9 Foundation vs those who didn't; Grade 12 results; post-school placement | Follow-up |

### What we ask of the department
1. Introductions to 3 pilot schools and their district support teams.
2. Research approval through the department's research process.
3. Guidance on exporting marks from SA-SAMS for consenting learners.
4. A contact for career-guidance policy (Life Orientation / psychosocial support).
5. A joint review of the pilot report in Term 4 2027.

### What we provide
The full product free for the pilot schools; a free session for every HOD and LO teacher first; training for LO teachers; safeguarding set-up; all reports and data analysis.

## Part C — Sunday with Thando: questions to ask

Ask these and write the answers down. They decide how the pilot gets designed.

1. **Subject-change window:** "Until when can a Grade 11 learner change a subject in Gauteng schools? Who approves it?" (The Redirect verdict depends on this.)
2. **Research approval:** "What's the process for getting a pilot approved in GDE schools, and how long does it take?"
3. **Who owns career guidance:** "Which unit or district team is responsible for subject choice and career guidance? Who should I meet?"
4. **Psychometrics policy:** "Does GDE have rules about which assessments or psychometric tools can be used with learners?" (Links to file 09 risk 2.)
5. **Existing tools:** "What do Gauteng schools currently use for subject choice and career guidance, and what do teachers complain about?"
6. **Marks data:** "Can schools export learner marks from SA-SAMS for a project like this, with consent?"
7. **Pilot schools:** "If I wanted three schools in one district for a free pilot next year, which district would you suggest?"
8. **Timing:** "When do Grade 9 subject choices usually close in Gauteng schools?"
9. **His role:** "Would you be open to being an informal adviser on this while I set it up?"

### How to run the conversation
- Open with the problem, not the product: *"How do Grade 9s in your schools actually choose subjects?"* Let him talk first.
- Show the **Lerato sample** (file 08) on your phone: the Grade 9 report, then the Grade 11 report. That two-year story is the pitch.
- End with one small, specific ask: an introduction to the right person, or one school for Term 1.
